Privacy

Last updated: October 26, 2026

Your Data and Choices

Your editing sessions, their history, your settings and stored assets stay in this browser’s storage. Files go only where you save them. The web editor works without accounts, cookies or crash reports.

The editor sends usage statistics, such as which tools you use and how long actions take, to a server we run. Sending starts only after your first interaction, such as clicking or pressing a key. Turn off Settings › About › Send Usage Statistics to stop sending immediately.

If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, statistics are off unless you turn the setting on. Your choice applies to this browser only. Clearing the site’s data resets it. Statistics are then on again unless your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

Who Runs This

%PRODUCT_NAME% is an editor for the Next SEKAI engine in Sonolus. It is an open-source project by qwewqa. qwewqa runs this site and is responsible for the use of personal data described here.

Send privacy questions and requests to nse@qwewqa.dev. Report bugs on the GitHub issues page.

Data in Your Browser

BGM, preview audio and covers are stored with the sessions that use them. When space runs low, the editor may automatically delete audio and covers used only by earlier Timeline steps. This cleanup is limited to those audio and cover files. Your browser may also clear this site’s storage. Settings › Local Data says when.

Skins and other preview assets loaded from files are also stored only in this browser and remain under their authors’ terms.

To delete sessions, use Settings › Local Data › Delete Sessions… or History › Recent Sessions. Discard recovered work in Settings › Local Data, and remove stored assets in Preview Assets › Stored. Deleted sessions may remain in browser storage until automatic cleanup runs. Clearing the site’s data in your browser deletes all data stored there.

Usage Statistics

We use usage statistics to improve the editor. Our legal basis for collecting and using them is our legitimate interest in improving the editor. The information sent is limited to the measurements below. Your charts and their content, file names, level titles, other text you type and identifiers stored in your browser stay on your device.

  • Tools and Settings. How often each tool, command and kind of edit is used, and how it was started, such as from a key, the toolbar or a menu. This includes the main settings, such as theme, language and whether the minimap is shown, and how a visit started, such as recovering earlier work. For notices and errors, the editor sends counts only.
  • Files and Charts. The kind and approximate size of files you open, save, export and check, what a save included, whether each action worked, and the approximate length of music files. The editor also sends the approximate chart size, such as “250 to 319 notes”, whether the chart has music, how many steps the Undo window holds, and how many sessions Recent Sessions lists. Checks include whether Check File and History found problems and whether the check included a file as well as History.
  • Performance. How the page was loaded, such as by a reload or from the browser’s cache, whether it was hidden during startup, and whether the browser had discarded it earlier. Measurements include how long startup takes; how long it takes to open, save, export and check files and load music; how long a file takes to prepare for saving or export; and how long the chart takes to show at a Timeline step. The editor also sends how often it is slow to respond, the number of clicks and key presses, and which performance measurements the browser supports. Time spent with the editor visible and time spent playing while it is visible are sent in seconds, up to one day.
  • Browser and Device. The editor’s version, whether it was updated since your last visit, and whether this browser has used it before. This includes your operating system, browser and its language, screen size and pixel density, approximate window size and whether it is taller than wide. The browser supplies the number of processor cores, approximate memory and whether your main way of pointing is imprecise, such as a finger on a touch screen. The editor also sends whether the preview could use graphics and draw in parallel, and whether the editor is running in a window of its own.
  • Storage. Whether this browser’s storage is available, its approximate capacity and how full it is, whether the browser has granted protection from automatic clearing, and whether automatic cleanup ran, freed space or was unable to finish.
  • Referring Sites. If you followed a link from another site, the editor sends its protocol, domain and any non-default port.

The editor sends these statistics in reports. Each report includes a timestamp. For one action, this is when it happened. For a summary, it is when the summary was made. Measurements of startup, files, music, Timeline steps and slow responses also include the browser, pointing precision, window size, pixel density, processor cores and memory described above. These details are read when a file or music action ends, or when statistics about startup, Timeline steps or slow responses are prepared.

Counts, such as tool uses and the number of sessions in Recent Sessions, are sent as whole numbers up to 9999. The number of steps in the Undo window is sent exactly. Startup and file or music action times are sent in whole milliseconds, up to ten minutes per measurement. The time to show a Timeline step is sent as a range. Screen size is sent as it is. Processor core counts from 1 to 32 and pixel densities of 1, 2 and 3 are sent exactly; higher core counts and other pixel densities are sent as ranges. Chart object counts below 10 are sent exactly. Larger counts, music lengths and file sizes are sent as ranges. Together these figures can sometimes match a published chart.

If your browser is too old to open the editor, the page that tells you so sends the browser family, such as Firefox or Safari, and an indication that the browser is unsupported. It follows your statistics setting and the browser signals described above, and sends after your first interaction with that page, such as clicking, pressing a key, touching or scrolling.

Counting Visits

With each request, the server receives your IP address and user agent, the information that your browser sends about itself. It uses them to count visits and find your country, browser, operating system and device type, such as phone or laptop. It may also find your region and city. Of these details, it stores only your country, browser, operating system and device type.

To count visits, the server keeps a daily visitor code made from the IP address and user agent. The same code is used for a browser’s activity within a day. People using the same browser type on the same connection can share a code. The statistics server stores its data only on the server.

Where Data Is Processed

The site at nse.qwewqa.dev consists of static files hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub may log requests, including your IP address, and keeps those logs under its own policy. See GitHub’s privacy statement.

The editor sends usage statistics only to stats.nse-services.qwewqa.dev. We process and store them on Amazon Web Services (AWS) servers in the United States, in the us-west-2 region. AWS provides hosting and backup storage and is the only provider with whom we share these statistics. We keep them separate from data from other sites. Only the maintainer has access to the statistics dashboard.

For transfers subject to EU data protection rules, we rely on the safeguards in the AWS Service Terms. These include the EU Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by the European Commission in June 2021. The clauses apply automatically to AWS customers for transfers covered by those terms.

How Long We Keep Data

We keep individual sets of usage statistics for 13 months. Before deleting them, we add their figures to monthly and weekly totals. We keep both kinds of totals with no time limit.

Monthly totals include visits, counts of daily visitor codes, reports of each kind, usage counts, settings, file types, totals of numeric measurements and typical action times. We group visits and counts of daily visitor codes separately by editor version, browser, operating system, device type, country and language. We group action times by editor version and device type.

Weekly totals count visits, distinct daily visitor codes and reports of each kind. We use ISO weeks, starting on Monday in UTC. Detailed breakdowns, totals of numeric measurements and action times are kept only in monthly totals.

Each group kept in monthly or weekly totals must cover at least 10 distinct daily visitor codes. Smaller groups are combined as “other” where possible, or omitted. These totals consist only of combined figures.

Backups on AWS are deleted after about five weeks. After an unexpected failure, server error logs may contain your IP address. We delete these logs after two weeks.

Email and Files You Send Us

When you contact us, we use your email address and message to answer you and keep the conversation in our mailbox. If you send a file to help fix a problem, we use it only for that and delete it when the problem is fixed.

A project file or a file from Settings › Local Data › Export Raw Data can contain your charts and audio. Send it by email to nse@qwewqa.dev and keep it out of public issues.

Your Rights

Depending on the law that applies, you may have rights to access, correct or delete your personal data, restrict its use, or object to its use. Use the email address above for questions and requests. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

Usage statistics use a daily visitor code in place of a name or account. We may therefore be unable to identify records that relate to you.
