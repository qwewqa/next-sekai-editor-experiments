Who Runs This

%PRODUCT_NAME% is an editor for the Next SEKAI engine in Sonolus. It is an open-source project by qwewqa.

qwewqa runs this site and is responsible for the use of personal data described here.

Contact: nse@qwewqa.dev. Report bugs on the GitHub issues page. Send privacy questions and requests by email.

The Short Version

The web editor has no accounts, cookies or crash reports. It sends usage statistics to our own server, such as which tools you use and how long actions take. Your charts and file names are excluded. Turn off Settings › About › Send Usage Statistics to stop sending statistics. The site itself is static files on GitHub Pages.

The Web Editor

  • Your editing sessions, their history, your settings and your stored assets stay in this browser’s storage. Files go only where you save them.
  • BGM, preview audio and covers are stored with the sessions that use them. When space runs low, audio and covers may be deleted if only earlier Timeline steps use them. Automatic deletion is limited to those audio and cover files. Your browser may also clear this site’s storage. Settings › Local Data says when.

Requests It Makes

  • Loading the site from nse.qwewqa.dev, hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub may log requests, including your IP address. See GitHub’s privacy statement.
  • Usage statistics to stats.nse-services.qwewqa.dev, unless you turn them off. See Usage Statistics.

Usage Statistics

We use usage statistics to improve the editor. Our legal basis for collecting and using them is our legitimate interest in improving the editor.

The editor sends usage statistics only to stats.nse-services.qwewqa.dev, a server we run. We process and store them on Amazon Web Services (AWS) servers in the United States, in the us-west-2 region. AWS provides hosting and backup storage and is the only provider with whom we share these statistics. We keep them separate from data from other sites. Only the maintainer has access to the statistics dashboard.

For transfers subject to EU data protection rules, we rely on the safeguards in the AWS Service Terms. These include the EU Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by the European Commission in June 2021. The clauses apply automatically to AWS customers for transfers covered by those terms.

The editor sends usage counts, settings, information about file and chart sizes, performance measurements, browser and device information, storage information and referring sites. The details are listed below.

Tools and Settings

  • How often each tool, command and kind of edit is used, and how it was started, such as from a key, the toolbar or a menu.
  • The main settings, such as the theme, the language and whether the minimap is shown.
  • How a visit started, for example whether earlier work was recovered, and counts of notices and errors. Their messages are excluded.

Files and Charts

  • The kind and approximate size of files you open, save, export and check, what a save included, and whether that worked.
  • The approximate length of music files.
  • The approximate size of the chart, such as “250 to 319 notes”, whether it has music, how many steps the Undo window holds, and how many sessions Recent Sessions lists.
  • Whether Check File and History found problems, and whether the check included a file as well as History.

Performance

  • How the page was loaded, such as by a reload or from the browser’s cache, whether it was hidden while it started, and whether the browser had discarded it earlier.
  • How long startup takes, how long it takes to open, save, export and check files and to load music, how long a file takes to prepare for saving or export, and how long the chart takes to show at a Timeline step.
  • How often the editor is slow to respond, how many clicks and key presses there were, and which performance measurements the browser supports.
  • How long the editor was visible and how long playback ran while it was, in seconds, up to one day.

Browser and Device

  • The editor’s version, whether it was updated since your last visit, and whether this browser has used it before.
  • Your operating system, browser and its language, your screen size and pixel density, an approximate window size and whether it is taller than wide.
  • Whether the browser reports that your main way of pointing is imprecise, such as a finger on a touch screen, and the number of processor cores and the approximate memory the browser reports.
  • Whether the preview could use graphics and draw in parallel, and whether the editor is running in a window of its own.

Storage

  • Whether this browser’s storage is available, its approximate capacity and how full it is, and whether the browser has granted protection from automatic clearing.
  • Whether automatic cleanup ran, freed space or was unable to finish.

Referring Sites

If you followed a link from another site, the editor sends that site’s domain only.

Measurement Details

Each report includes a timestamp. For one action, this is the time it happened. For a summary, it is the time the summary was made.

Measurements of startup, files, music, Timeline steps and slow responses also include your browser, whether your main way of pointing is imprecise, the window size, pixel density, processor cores and memory. For a file or music action, these are read when the action ends. For startup, Timeline steps and slow responses, they are read when the report is made.

Your charts and their content, file names, level titles, other text you type and identifiers stored in your browser are excluded from reports. Counts, such as how often a tool is used, are sent as numbers up to 9999. How long an action takes is sent in milliseconds, up to ten minutes. The screen size is sent as it is. Other numbers, such as sizes and how long the chart takes to show a step, are sent as ranges.

Chart object counts below 10 are sent exactly. Larger counts, music lengths and file sizes are sent as ranges. Together these figures can sometimes match a published chart.

Reports from a visit start only after you use the editor, for example by clicking or pressing a key. If your browser is too old to open the editor, the page that tells you so sends the browser family, such as Firefox or Safari, and a report that the browser is unsupported, when you click or press a key on it.

Counting Visits

The server receives your IP address and browser’s user agent with each request. It uses them to count visits and to find your country, browser, operating system and device type, such as phone or laptop. It may also find your region and city. Of these details, it stores only your country, browser, operating system and device type.

To count visits, the server keeps a daily code made from the IP address and user agent. The same code is used for one browser’s activity within a day. People on the same connection with the same browser can get the same code. The server sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser.

Monthly and Weekly Totals

Monthly totals include the number of visits, how often each tool, setting and file type is used, and typical times. Visits are counted separately by editor version, browser, operating system, device type, country and language. Times are counted by editor version and device type.

Weekly totals include visits, sessions and how often each event happens. They use ISO weeks, starting on Monday in UTC. Breakdowns and timings are kept only in the monthly totals.

In both monthly and weekly totals, a group is shown separately only if it contains at least 10 distinct daily visitor codes. Smaller groups are combined as “other”. If the combined group still contains fewer than 10 distinct daily visitor codes, it is omitted. Both kinds of totals contain only combined figures, with daily visitor codes and individual reports excluded.

Content from Files

Skins and other preview assets you load from a file are stored in this browser only. They stay under their authors’ terms.

How Long We Keep Data

  • Usage reports. We delete individual usage reports after 13 months. Before deletion, their figures are added to the monthly and weekly totals described above. We keep both kinds of totals with no time limit.
  • Backups. Backups on AWS are deleted after about five weeks.
  • Server error logs. These logs may contain your IP address after an unexpected failure. We delete them after two weeks.
  • GitHub’s logs. GitHub keeps its request logs under its own policy.

Email and Files You Send Us

If you contact us, we use your email address and message to answer you, and keep the conversation in our mailbox.

If you send us a file to help fix a problem, we use it only for that and delete it when the problem is fixed. This can be a project file or the file from Export Raw Data in Settings › Local Data. Send it by email to nse@qwewqa.dev. Keep it out of public issues. It contains your charts and audio.

Deleting Data in Your Browser

Delete sessions with Settings › Local Data › Delete Sessions… or in History › Recent Sessions. Discard recovered work under Settings › Local Data. Remove stored assets in Preview Assets › Stored. Deleted sessions may stay in browser storage until automatic cleanup runs.

Clearing the site’s data in your browser deletes all of it, including the statistics choice.

Your Rights and Choices

Turn off Settings › About › Send Usage Statistics to stop sending statistics immediately. If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, statistics are off unless you turn the setting on.

This choice applies to this browser only. Clearing the site’s data resets it. Statistics are then on again unless your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

Depending on the law that applies, you may have rights to access, correct or delete your personal data, restrict its use, or object to its use. Send questions and requests to nse@qwewqa.dev. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

Usage reports contain a daily visitor code rather than a name or account, so we may be unable to identify records that relate to you.
